Six proposed dates, one constraint list.

Scholars have put the Mahābhārata war anywhere from 5561 to 1198 BCE. Each proposal points at the sky. So: take one reader's verse-derived constraints — Saturn near Rohiṇī, Mars retrograde near Jyeṣṭhā, a lunar eclipse followed by a solar one within a fortnight — and run them, unchanged, against every proposed date.

These are computed when you load this page, not stored. The engine reaches no verdict. It prints what each proposal asked for and what the sky actually did.

What this does and does not show

Meeting a constraint is not proof, and failing one is not refutation — a proposal chooses the constraints it is judged by, and the constraint list used here belongs to one of the six. The sources are recorded in the repository beside each date, and where nobody has yet checked a citation against the original publication, it says so above rather than quietly implying it has been.

ΔT — the drift between Earth's rotation and uniform time — is about twenty-one hours at 3067 BCE and is known only to within tens of minutes before 720 BCE. That is enough to move what was rising, and far too little to move a planet out of its nakshatra, which is why longitude constraints carry no tolerance for it and rising ones would.
